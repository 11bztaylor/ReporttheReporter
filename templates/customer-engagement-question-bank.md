# Customer engagement questions — metrics as a roadmap, not an audit accusation

**Purpose:** Convert the long-term executive catalog into collaborative questions that help customers establish *useful security and resilience practices*. This is a suggestion bank, **not** a mandated questionnaire, regulatory audit, sales obligation or assumption that all MSSP customers subscribe to every capability.

| Exec theme | Introductory question | What we might jointly establish | Later evidence that could justify mature reporting |
|---|---|---|---|
| E01 Visibility | "What systems or services do you expect us to help monitor?" | Agreed scope, owners, eligible cohorts and exclusions | Reconciled expected-source population and validated observations |
| E02 Provider service | "What monitoring or notification expectations have already been agreed?" | Service commitments and provider/customer handoffs | Demonstrated performance against agreed service objectives |
| E03 Customer readiness | "Who can help verify our customer/service mapping and priorities?" | Named scope steward, contact process, review record | Validated applicability and resolved attribution disputes |
| E04 Mission exposure | "Which disruptions would materially harm your mission?" | Business-owned criticality, scenarios and compensating controls | Authorized risk assessment grounded in validated conditions |
| E05 Exploited exposure | "How are high-priority exposed assets and patch exceptions handled?" | Asset applicability and remediation decision path | Repeated validated remediation and exposure duration |
| E06 Detection/response | "Which threats and response handoffs matter most to your operations?" | Agreed use cases, responder roles, clocks and test plan | Controlled detection/response exercises and comparable incident outcomes |
| E07 Identity | "Who owns privileged access and how is it reviewed?" | Eligible privileged cohort, baseline and exceptions | Verified protection and lifecycle control outcomes |
| E08 Data protection | "What data loss or application compromise would matter most?" | Data owners, classification scope and applicable defenses | Validated consequential data protection and remediation outcomes |
| E09 Public-facing protection | "Which public services must stay available and protected?" | Application owners, edge service scope and protection expectations | Tested WAF/DDoS controls, service continuity and corrective outcomes |
| E10 Recovery | "Which services would be most important to restore first?" | Critical service owner, recovery objectives and exercise ownership | Recorded restoration times, integrity tests and approved objectives |
| E11 Dependencies | "Which suppliers or shared services would be hard to operate without?" | Material third-party owners, common dependencies and response contacts | Validated material dependence and mitigation exercises |
| E12 Outcomes | "How should we know whether a change actually helped?" | Decision owner, success criterion and next review | Validated before/after outcomes with known scope changes |

## Suggested conversational sequence

1. **Lead with our actual observation and its limit.** "We have this kind of event evidence; we cannot yet confirm full service scope."
2. **Ask only the smallest useful question.** "Who can verify which public-facing services are expected?"
3. **Explain what the answer unlocks.** "That helps us avoid claiming protection for systems outside the agreed service."
4. **Offer a reasonable next step.** "We can begin with the most critical service instead of requesting your entire inventory at once."
5. **Record the result without making a grading claim.** Customer declaration, provider observation, confirmed outcome or unvalidated next action.

A customer can legitimately decline, defer, or identify a capability as not applicable. Preserve the decision and rationale; do **not** equate lack of a new optional exercise with a failing customer security score. The MSSP can identify and explain the opportunity; the authorized customer/business function owns recovery objectives and risk decisions.

## Sample: recovery readiness without premature RTO metrics

**Initial:** "We haven't seen recovery-test evidence, and log data isn't enough to tell us whether services could recover. Which business services would be most disruptive if unavailable?"

**Developing:** "Is there a designated owner for recovery priorities? Would an existing continuity plan or a small tabletop exercise be a useful starting point?"

**Mature:** "When your team has approved recovery objectives and test results, we can report whether the tested services achieved those objectives and which improvements still need attention."

**Do not say:** "Your recovery performance is 0%." The defensible early finding is **not measured yet**, with an actionable capability-development question.

## Minimal request record

Theme/metric ID · Customer/capability context · Actual evidence and as-of date · Question asked · Reason it matters · Customer response and provenance · Proposed owner · Next follow-up · Updated measurement readiness.

No brand, ingestion pipeline, portal access model, or technical asset collection method is prescribed here.
