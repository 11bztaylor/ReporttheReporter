# One-page leadership briefing

## Requested decision
**Sponsor a 90-day pilot of an Enterprise Cybersecurity Measurement & Decision Support Program.**

## The recurring problem
Leadership often requests 'metrics'; teams respond with custom dashboards. Without standing definitions, governance, trustworthy coverage information, action ownership, and follow-up, dashboards become stale and rarely influence investment or risk decisions.

## Our proposed change
**Technicians** collect and verify evidence → **the program** translates it into reviewed measurements and decision packets → **Reporting Unit leaders** validate mission impacts and act on local barriers → **enterprise leaders** handle cross-Reporting Unit, funding, policy and risk choices → **the program** measures whether action worked.

## Why this differs from another dashboard
- Starts with decision questions, not whatever numbers happen to be available.
- Same evidence underpins technical, program, Reporting Unit and executive views.
- Coverage and uncertainty are reported alongside findings.
- A momentary count is not automatically a persistent risk.
- Reporting Unit challenges, method changes and snapshots remain auditable.
- Decisions, ownership and outcomes are recorded and revisited.

## Standards grounding
NIST CSF 2.0 defines outcomes; NIST SP 800-55 Vols. 1–2 guides measures and program establishment; SP 800-137 supports continuous monitoring. CIS Controls Assessment Specification offers measurement building blocks. CISA CDM demonstrates government enterprise reporting at scale.

## Pilot scope
2–3 volunteer Reporting Units; 3 measures: vulnerability remediation, endpoint protection coverage, security telemetry coverage; use existing tooling where practical (vulnerability tools, endpoint protection platform, security monitoring platform, historical analytics store and business intelligence reporting layer).

## Support requested
**Executive sponsor · Named program owner · Risk/GRC participation · Reporting Unit scope/asset SME access · Technical implementation time · Recurring decision review**

## What success looks like
By the pilot's end, demonstrate traceable and contested/validated evidence, a repeatable measurement, an Reporting Unit-facing interpretation, one documented decision, and a scheduled outcome review—not just a dashboard screenshot.

### Thirty-second introduction
'We keep answering metrics requests with dashboards, but a dashboard by itself doesn't tell leadership what decision to make. I'd like us to adopt the NIST measurement and continuous monitoring guidance as a real program. Engineers would deliver defensible evidence; the program would validate and translate it; Reporting Unit and executive leaders would see the decisions at their level; and we'd track whether action improved security. I'm asking for sponsorship of a limited pilot rather than approval for another report.'
