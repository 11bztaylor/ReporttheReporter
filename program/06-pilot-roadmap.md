# 90-day pilot — measurement program, not implementation project

**Proposed evaluation.** The goal is to demonstrate trustworthy MSSP measurement and three-level communication from inherited observations, **not** to promise dashboards, integrations, enterprise scores or customer-wide deployment.

## Days 1–30 — Discovery and decision framing

- Assign a program sponsor, measurement lead, a provider service representative and customer relationship contacts as available.
- Select **E01 (what we can see)**, **E02 (what our evidence can support)** and **E03 (what must be validated)** as starting executive questions; keep other themes visible but explicitly *not assessable*.
- Describe available inherited evidence types, source recency and representative usability using [Phase 0 cards](../metrics/starter-metric-cards.md).
- Document uncertain customer attribution, unknown service commitments, missing scope, limitations and validation requests.
- Review initial reporting language with leadership; do not publish fleet coverage or SLA attainment without a verified basis.

**Exit:** documented observations, confidence and limitations, first customer/prior-service validation questions, initial executive/manager/technical narratives.

## Days 31–60 — Customer and service applicability

- With available customer representatives, validate source attribution, customer-defined assets/critical services and subscribed service expectations **where possible**.
- Decide which candidate measures can be promoted to scoped measurement and which must remain discovery-only.
- Specify approved definitions (cohort, numerator/denominator if used, source quality and freshness, competing explanations).
- Distinguish provider-correctable measurement or service faults from customer-owned exposure and shared questions.
- Record and resolve at least one evidence dispute or customer challenge.

**Exit:** approved metric cards for a limited customer/capability cohort; unapproved metrics clearly marked; no universal customer security score.

## Days 61–90 — Decision and outcome demonstration

- Present one evidence-backed, proportionate decision request or explicitly reasoned decision to continue monitoring.
- Show that executive, management and technical views agree on the same evidence and limitations.
- Name action authority, proposed next step and an outcome review date. Do not precommit purchases from data alone.
- Document the result and revise the metric definition if the evidence was not sufficient.
- Make a go/revise/stop recommendation about *program usefulness*, not production readiness.

**Exit:** reproducible reporting and a decision/outcome trail. An executive may make an informed "insufficient evidence, validate first" decision; that is a valid pilot outcome.

## Stop / revise / proceed

- **Proceed:** the chosen question is decision-useful and the published conclusion matches validated evidence.
- **Revise:** sources are useful but customer scope, outcome definitions, ownership or uncertainty language needs improvement.
- **Stop a specific metric:** a proposed number would mislead, cannot be tied to a decision, or requires an unverified denominator. Do not halt the *entire program* merely because some customer environments are not ready.

## Track success

Use [program health](09-outcome-review-and-program-health.md) to monitor corrected claims, clear provider/customer responsibility, useful validation requests, decision follow-through, and metric retirement. Do not use a count of charts built as program success.

Technical procurement, integration, dashboard permissions and data-engineering timelines are **separate workstreams**.
